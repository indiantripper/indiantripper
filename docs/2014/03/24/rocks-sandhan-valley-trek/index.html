On the Rocks - Sandhan Valley Trek

By HarshMarch 24, 20146 min read

I started this year with a promise to myself that I would travel as many places as I could. It hasn't been going exactly how it was planned but still every now and then I feel content and not guilty at wasting my weekend. Our group has split up post NITIE (quite obviously), many of the others who stayed are too busy with their office, and the rest ... the rest are either getting engaged or married (Full Stop for most of the bro-outings :P).

Anyways, this fine weekend I decided to go for the Sandhan Valley trek along with an amazing set of people whom I didn't knew. The known strangers, thanks to Mumbai Travellers. Just for the info and creating an impression that I know everything, Sandhan valley (famously know as the "Valley of Shadow" - sounds spooky isn't it :P) is a canyon located in the Western Ghats. Starting from Mumbai via the last local, we (Me plus three strangers who bonded together to form another version of Chaai Sutta Chronicles by the end of the journey) reached the last stop, Kasara, around 3 in the morning. After a lot of efforts, our lead guide managed to gather us all together at one place and took our attendance (I felt like giving a proxy for someone but unfortunately, everyone had turned up :P).

7 jeeps were waiting outside to carry us all and dump into this beautiful village which was meant to serve as our pit stop. The list of essentials that we were provided with should have mentioned sweater as well, since it was really cold outside. Being a little stuffed in the jeep came out to be a blessing afterall. It also meant that we could grab some sleep since there was no risk of recociating around the vehicle.

Samrad Village. 6 AM in the morning. There was beauty. There was serenity. And there was peace. It was beautiful, and we knew that an awesome day ahead was waiting for us. Everyone was all set to go ahead. Photographers with their weapons loaded. Animal lovers chasing cats, hens and other accomplices. And the bros all set to light the stick up. Until they found that they need to perform the morning ritual in the fields, in the open. A decision was made not to risk it, until a viable solution was found. Having spent all my childhood summer holidays in the village, I perfectly knew what to do. Strategy was in place. Find a mug (or a mug-like stuff), a tree fort and set off. Like a tiger, everyone marked their territory and the first mission was accomplished. Terrorists won this time around and the stick was lit to celebrate the victory :D :D

Few more places were explored, breakfast was put to good use and everyone gathered around half past 7 to set off. But still we needed to wait a little more. Few of our group mates were "getting ready" ;) . Anyways, everyone joined in and post introduction, the troop marched on. Guidelines were clear. Stick together like a team. Be alert of the monkeys who might trigger a rock fall. No smoking. And most important, be sure to differentiate between the snacks and the snakes.

Making our way through the trees, we entered the valley of shadows. The rock castle. "On the rocks" has always been familiar to me, but not in this way. This was something else and it was sheer wonderful. A narrow alley full of rocks led into a deeper territory and we kept marching on. From rock to rock, from section to section, and from land to water and back to land. Ever heard of a natural air conditioner? It was one. It was like one of those deep secret mysterious place that you hear about in stories. For a good part of the day, we were in the another world.

The trek, as wonderful as it was, was marked by a lot of highs. The first one being crossing the water pool that crossed our path in the first half of the trek. The pool in the rocks, the wind blowing a whistle, and the water singing - drip drop drip drop drop drop drop. All the stuff packed in the bag, shoes tied to the bag, trouser folded up everyone set into it one by one. The water was clear. The water was cold. The water was our companion.

Some time passed by, we were wondering if the presence of snakes was told just to add on some flavor to the already set in ecstacy. Just then, something fell from the top. Followed by another sound which turned into a kind of stone ripple which was progressing forward. The monkeys were here. Our guide didn't waste a second to let us know what needs to be done. To the walls. We looked up. I saw a white creature running along. It was a majestic langoor, running flawlessly along the almost vertical walls. Wonderful sight it was.

Meeting other groups, slipping at times, cautioning others about the same, we reached the end of the half-trek, from where we were to take a U Turn. But not before we made the most of this place. Reached the top, the view of entire valley was fantabulous. We sat there, Nature was communicating with us. We were at peace. Snacks time, and everyone was passing their stuff around. All the time we keep complaining that this world is selfish and cruel. But there we were, strangers to each other, and in those few hours we had bonded like anything. Happiness brings the best out of you. And that's what this trip was all about. In fact that's what all such trips are all about.

We returned through the same path. While waiting for others, we socialized even more. And the chickens around that place helped us a lot in this. Killing time, chasing them all around, promising to eat them to terrorize them even more, it was some fun :P It was followed by lunch, a chicken-free lunch, but amazing nonetheless. After all we were tired and hungry, and it was completely different to what we have been in habit of eating. Bajre ki roti, arhar ki daal, baigan ki sabji, kaanda and achaar. Bliss !!!

We returned after some time, to an Indian match at home. It was a great Sunday. A rare hangover-free Sunday, but I am so glad that it happened. Thanks to everyone who was a part of it. Cheers !!!!
